#4471 is three problems, not one

owner support desk open reviewed 2026-10-28 · 0 days ago

The #4471 page says: one issue, known, one fix queued. That is true for most of it and wrong for the rest. The reports in #4471 hold three different problems. One fix closes the first one; the other two would stay open, marked known, with nobody looking at them.

Do not close the reports in #4471 when the parser fix ships. Two other problems are inside it.

How we told them apart

The cause of a report comes from where it came from, not from what intake guessed:

  1. Which tenant sent it, and does their locale write the day first? (causes.ts asks the platform; no list is typed.)
  2. Which booking form does the tenant run, and which way does it tell people to type the date?
  3. Does the report describe a day and month swapped, or a date moved by exactly one day?

meta.intakeTag is a guess from the reporter’s browser at the moment the report arrived. The table counts how often it points to the wrong problem. Do not triage on it.

Why they were merged as one

Decision 0001 merges on the subject line alone. All three problems share the same few subjects (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Dates mixed up”), and a reporter’s subject says nothing about the tenant or the form. The decision accepted the risk that one bug ends up in several tickets. Here it did the opposite: one ticket for three bugs, which is harder to see.

Triage in duplicates tells the desk to merge when unsure. For a flipped date, read the tenant and the channel first.

Since April

What to do

  • Fix the parser to read dates in the tenant’s locale, and close only the parser ticket.
  • Change the booking-v3 hint to the tenant’s order, or replace it with a date picker.
  • Reproduce the one-day shift near midnight in a time zone ahead of UTC. Do not assume it is the parser.
  • Replace decision 0001 with a record that takes the tenant and channel into account. An accepted decision is never edited, so this is a new record.

Lessons

  • A big ticket is often several bugs reported many times. Count each cause once, not the ticket once.
  • When the cause comes from tenant data, the tag from intake cannot be the source of truth.
  • A small group hidden in a big ticket stays “known” until someone splits it. Five reports are worth a ticket.
